## Game Concept

<!-- Explain why the working title fits. Mention alternative titles if useful. -->
While the name is super generic, I wanted it to be simple, as the point of the game is just as simple, essentially golfing while trying to ruin your friends' golfing. I thought about renaming it to Get Golfed, but adding the "Get" didn't make sense to me. That choice was purely based on my own feelings and not any real proof.

<!-- Describe the setting, theme, and story or premise. Identify the characters
or the role the player takes. A game without a story can describe its situation
and player roles instead; do not invent a narrative just to fill this section. -->
The premise of the game is to play golf, but the other players can add to the course/field. Your place as a player is to make the golf course easy enough for you to beat in as few strokes as possible, while making it hard for your friends to beat it. As a party game, a lot of the fun is up to the players to make.

## Gameplay

<!-- Explain the player's objectives, the conflicts or challenges, and how play
succeeds, fails, or ends. Describe the recurring decisions and actions, the rules
that connect them, and what changes in the game as a result. Game state means the
current situation, such as positions, resources, score, or available choices.
Describe the intended experience through those interactions. -->

The player's goal is to play the course built by the other players, turn by turn, in as few strokes as possible, just like regular golf. The challenges you then face are the placement of the course tiles and getting knocked away by the other players in the scenario that their ball hits your ball. The ending occurs after a set number of placement turns have happened; when it occurs, the player with the fewest strokes wins.

The rules of the base game are the exact same as golf. When it comes to placing tiles or obstacles on the map, you only get to choose one, and after all players have received their tiles or obstacles, they must add them to the map. Once they have been placed, the game returns to the rules of golf until all players have made it to the end/have taken too many strokes.

### Setup And Start

<!-- Describe the starting state: how the play area and pieces are arranged,
what each player begins with, and how play starts. Refer to the pieces described
below rather than listing them twice. -->

The game starts with the players selecting their first tiles. The number of tiles to choose from is the same as the number of players currently playing. Once the players have chosen their tiles, they get to add it on to the course and the goal tile is attached. Every turn from now on will start by removing the end piece, selecting tiles or obstacles, and then reattaching everything.

### Core Gameplay Loop

<!-- Describe the recurring player choices and actions, the rules that connect
them, and what changes as a result. Game state is the current situation, such as
positions, resources, score, or available choices. -->

The main decision players will face is where to putt their ball, and what tiles/obstacles to choose from in the selection phase. Everyone else's actions will be decided by what you select and where you decide to place it. 

### Ending And Outcome

<!-- State when play ends and how the outcome is decided. Include win/lose
conditions where applicable, or another clear beginning/end structure. -->

The game ends once the allocated number of turns have happened. While the players can't fully lose, you are ranked by how many strokes you had by the end of the game; the player with the least gets first place, the person with the second least gets second, and this pattern continues down the list for the number of people playing.

For the physical prototype, there are only a few required things. You'll need the start and end tiles, the custom attachable tiles and obstacles, a score sheet, a ball to putt, and some kind of stick to hit the golf ball. The tiles and obstacles are necessary to have the dynamic play area, and the golf ball and stick are needed to play on that course. The scoreboard would be used to tally the number of strokes each player has done and see who gets first place.

The play area would consist of the starting and end tiles on the floor (the course can get pretty big with a lot of players). You then have a zone off to the side where your usable tiles and obstacles are placed. You place your ball on the starting tile along with your fellow players, and the game begins.
